A large polling-rate number is not necessarily the setting a mouse uses when it first reaches your desk. That distinction matters with the Black wired HyperX Pulsefire Haste 2. Its advertising calls attention to 8000Hz, while the manufacturer's support note says the default is 1000Hz. This buying checklist separates the two before you choose a model.

We have not used this mouse or measured its tracking, latency or comfort. The facts below come from HyperX support and HP's product information. PCMag's review supplies an independent perspective, but its reviewed sample was white and wired, not the exact Black unit linked here. Its observations are not our test results.

## Is 8000Hz switched on by default?
No. HyperX's specification footnote states that the default polling rate is 1000Hz. It says you can use an onboard button combination to switch to 8000Hz or configure the rate with HyperX NGENUITY. The headline is therefore a supported maximum, not a promise that every computer starts at that setting.

Decide whether you want to change settings at all before paying attention to the largest number. A mouse reporting more often is not evidence that a particular game, display or computer will deliver a matching improvement. This page does not predict a frame-rate increase, a latency reduction or a competitive advantage for your system. Those would need measurements we have not made.

## Which Haste 2 does this listing cover?
This page covers the Amazon option Black, Wired, Pulsefire Haste 2. HyperX's support table assigns SKU 6N0A7AA to Black and a different SKU to White. The same family also contains a wireless product; neither its connection methods nor its charging arrangements should be imported into this wired checklist.

Read all three option labels when you open the product page, rather than stopping after the family name. Mini, Core, Pro and other similarly named styles can appear nearby. A shared product-page family is not proof that those variants have the same cable, software features or polling-rate behavior. Match the complete style and connection before comparing specifications.

## What connection and cable must your desk accommodate?
HyperX specifies USB-A and a HyperFlex 2 cable, with a listed cable length of 1.8m. It is a wired input device, so a clear path between the mouse area and an available computer port matters. The manufacturer's support list names Windows 11 and Windows 10 for operating-system compatibility.

Check the port on the computer you actually intend to use. A laptop with only a different connector requires its own appropriate connection arrangement; no adapter is promised here. Leave enough freedom for your normal mouse movement instead of routing the lead so tightly that it pulls across the mat. This is a placement check, not a claim that this site measured cable drag.

## How many settings travel in the mouse?
HyperX lists six buttons and one onboard memory profile. NGENUITY can configure settings, and the official footnote specifically connects it to lighting customization and polling-rate selection. One onboard profile should not be confused with a large library of profiles all stored in the mouse itself.

If you alternate between games or shared computers, write down which settings you need without software running. PCMag discusses the distinction between software-managed profiles and onboard storage, but the current HyperX table is the authority for the one-profile specification used here. Our [mouse shape and sensitivity guide](/guides/gaming-mouse-shape-sensitivity/) helps organize the settings and fit questions separately.

## Will the shape and controls suit your hand?
A specification sheet cannot settle comfort. PCMag describes the shell, left-side thumb controls and limited lighting, while noting that hand size changes the grip decision. Its conclusions concern the reviewer's sample and hands, not a guarantee that the Black option fits everyone.

The official tables and promotional copy also present weight differently, so this checklist does not turn a rounded headline weight into an exact measurement for your unit. Start with the connection, button layout and return conditions you can verify. If your priority is many thumb shortcuts or several onboard profiles, six buttons and one profile are important limits rather than minor footnotes.

## Who should skip it, and what comes next?
Skip this wired model if you need Bluetooth, a rechargeable wireless mouse or a cable-free travel setup. Also pause if your main reason is a guaranteed performance improvement from 8000Hz: no such result has been established for your computer here. If you cannot use the required software on a managed computer, check the onboard controls before relying on a customized setup.

Before ordering, match Black, Wired and Pulsefire Haste 2; confirm a suitable USB connection and cable path; then decide whether one profile and six buttons are enough. Our [display resolution and input guide](/guides/monitor-resolution-refresh-input/) provides context for the rest of the setup. Keep the mouse decision tied to your actual desk and controls, not to one maximum specification.
