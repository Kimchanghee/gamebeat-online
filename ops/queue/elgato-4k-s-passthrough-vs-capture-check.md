A capture card has two jobs that are easy to blur together: sending the game to your screen, and sending a copy to your computer. For the Elgato 4K S, the purchase question is which of those two limits your setup will hit first. A listing headline with several resolutions does not tell you whether your recording, your screen, or both will use them.

This checklist uses Elgato product and support pages and the exact Amazon product page. We have not used this capture card, measured latency, or compared recorded footage with another model. It does not quote a price, discount, stock level or customer rating, and it is a buying checklist rather than a performance test.

## What is the difference between passthrough and capture on the 4K S?
Passthrough is the video that leaves the HDMI output toward your TV or monitor, while capture is the signal your computer receives over USB. Elgato describes the HDMI 2.0 output as lag-free passthrough and lists it up to 2160p60 and 1080p240, with variable refresh rate and HDR support. The capture list is shorter and is tied to the software and USB connection you use.

That means a console setting that looks smooth on the display may not be what ends up in a recording. Decide first whether you mostly want to play on a fast screen or to record and stream. If recording is the main goal, read the capture list before the passthrough list. The [capture card guide](/guides/capture-card-hdmi-usb-hdcp/) explains the HDMI, USB and copy-protection basics behind those two paths.

## Which capture resolutions does Elgato list?
Elgato lists capture up to 2160p60, and its product overview names 4K60 as the maximum capture. It also shows 1080p60, 720p60, 576p and 480p. Some other numbers in Elgato's own articles differ from one another, such as the highest 1440p capture rate, so this checklist leaves those figures out instead of choosing one.

If you record at 4K60, remember that the computer and storage also have to handle the file. Elgato's pages describe a USB 3.0 Type-C connection, and its detailed table separates what is available on USB 3.2 Gen 1 from what is available on USB 2.0. Check which port you will actually plug into before relying on the highest setting.

## What does HDR support really cover?
Elgato separates HDR passthrough from HDR capture. HDR 10-bit passthrough is listed up to 4K60, which concerns the signal sent to your display. HDR capture is listed up to 1080p60, and Elgato says it is currently available only on Windows.

So a Mac user or a 4K recording plan should not assume the HDR path is the same as the display path. Decide whether HDR recording matters at all. If you mainly record standard-range video at 4K60, the HDR note is a limit to understand rather than a deciding feature. Confirm the capture software and operating system you will use against the current Elgato support pages.

## What connections and sources should you check before buying?
Check the HDMI side first. Elgato lists HDMI 2.0 input, described as unencrypted, and HDMI 2.0 output for passthrough. It also lists a 3.5 mm stereo line-in for analog audio. The Amazon page names PS5, Xbox Series X and S, and Switch 2 as supported sources.

Write down the source device, its output setting, your display's input, and the cable between each part. A mismatch in any one of them can limit the result even when the card itself supports the listed formats. If a source blocks capture through copy protection, the card cannot change that, so read the console's own setting notes as well as the capture card page.

## Who should choose a different capture card?
Choose another model if you need a card that captures more than the 4K S lists, or if your source cannot pass its signal through HDMI 2.0. Someone who only wants to play on a screen without recording does not need a capture card at all.

The older Elgato HD60 X is a separate product. Elgato's own comparison lists its maximum capture as 4K30, and the maker's product page marks it as discontinued, so do not mix its figures into this checklist. Features written for another Elgato model should not silently become specifications of the 4K S.

## What should you write down before ordering?
Write down the exact product name, the console or computer you will connect, the display you will use, and the USB port you will use. Add whether you want recording, streaming, or both, and whether HDR recording is a requirement. Then compare that list with Elgato's current passthrough and capture tables.

If every item fits, this is a candidate worth comparing. If one item is unclear, such as the USB port or the operating system for HDR capture, settle it before ordering. The goal is a card whose listed limits match the signal you plan to send, not a box that surprises you after setup.
