The headline on a mouse can describe one connection rather than everything the mouse does. With the Slate Gray HyperX Pulsefire Haste 2 Pro 4K, that distinction changes both the polling-rate ceiling and the charging plan. This checklist is for a buyer who switches between a desktop receiver, a travel laptop and a charging cable.

We have not used this mouse or measured its input delay, tracking or battery. HyperX's current specifications and footnotes supply the numbers below. TechRadar and How-To Geek provide independent views about fit and controls; their experiences are attributed to those reviewers rather than presented as results from this site.

## Does wired mode keep the 4000Hz headline?
No. HyperX's footnote reserves 4kHz polling for the 2.4GHz connection. It lists a wired maximum of 1kHz and a Bluetooth maximum of 125Hz. Plugging in a cable to charge therefore does not mean that the wireless maximum automatically becomes the wired maximum too.

Keep these modes separate when comparing models. The non-Pro wired Haste 2 has its own specifications and is not interchangeable with this Pro Wireless listing. A family name shared by several mice is not enough to carry over a number. Read Grey, Wireless and Pulsefire Haste 2 Pro together on the selected option.

Polling rate is also not a measured end-to-end delay for your game. A reporting frequency does not tell us how your display, game engine or computer will behave. This article makes no frame-rate promise and does not turn a maximum into a competitive advantage. Use it to choose an appropriate connection before tuning a system.

## Does the longest battery figure apply at 4000Hz?
No. HyperX rates the mouse for up to 90 hours at 1000Hz and up to 30 hours at 4000Hz. The footnote describes continuous usage in 2.4GHz mode with lighting disabled, and says runtime varies with usage and computing conditions. Both figures are manufacturer ratings, not our measured charging intervals.

This is a meaningful choice if you dislike interrupting a desk session to recharge. Decide which polling setting you intend to use before judging the longest battery claim. The difference between the two ratings is not evidence of your own daily runtime, and neither value is a guarantee for another lighting setting or workload.

How-To Geek described strong battery behavior in its own use at 1000Hz. That historical observation supports checking the setting, not promising the same result for every owner. A short session on Bluetooth and a long session with the receiver should not be treated as the same test condition.

## What must you reserve space for on the desk?
HyperX lists a USB 4K wireless receiver, USB extension adapter and USB-C to USB-A cable in the box, along with grips and low-friction skates. Its current description also lists Bluetooth and play-and-charge over USB-C. Plan the connection around the actual host ports and the mode you need.

A receiver on a desk and a charging cable routed behind it solve different problems. Leave a practical cable path for charging without assuming that cable mode has the highest polling ceiling. The product page describes Instant Pair on compatible OMEN laptops, but this article does not generalize that feature to every laptop bearing the OMEN name.

Our [mouse shape and sensitivity guide](/guides/gaming-mouse-shape-sensitivity/) separates settings from physical fit. The receiver does not change the dimensions of the shell, and a connection specification does not settle whether the side buttons fall naturally under your thumb. Work through both questions before choosing.

## Do the shape and six controls match your priorities?
HyperX lists a symmetrical shape, six buttons, one onboard memory profile and a HyperX 26K sensor. Its specification table gives dimensions of 124.3 by 66.8 by 38.2mm. Those are useful reference dimensions, but this page has not measured a unit or judged your hand position.

TechRadar liked the build and multiple connections, while warning that the shape would not suit every grip and that its software lacked some advanced tweaks. How-To Geek viewed the low-profile design as a better match for smaller hands and was less enthusiastic about the side buttons and small wheel. These are separate reviewers' judgments, not a unanimous comfort result.

The practical question is which controls you need to reach, not whether an adjective such as Pro sounds appropriate. One onboard profile is a limit if you expect many hardware-stored configurations. Check current NGENUITY support for the computer on which you intend to configure it; we have not installed or tested the software here.

## Who should skip it, and what should a buyer check next?
Skip this model if your non-negotiable requirement is 4000Hz while wired or if Bluetooth must offer the same reporting rate as the receiver. Also reconsider if you need many thumb buttons or several onboard profiles, because the official list is six buttons and one profile. A higher model name cannot fill those gaps.

Before ordering, match the Slate Gray Pro Wireless model, choose the intended connection, decide which rated battery condition matters and check a suitable USB port. Our [monitor resolution and input guide](/guides/monitor-resolution-refresh-input/) helps place the mouse specification in the wider setup. None of these checks proves a performance improvement; they help avoid choosing on a headline that describes a different mode.
