The wired Razer DeathAdder V3 is an easy mouse to describe and a harder one to match to a hand. Razer's page gives a clear set of facts: a right-handed ergonomic shape, a wired connection, polling up to 8000 Hz, five onboard profiles and six programmable buttons. This checklist turns those facts into the questions that decide whether it fits your desk.

We have not used this mouse. Everything below comes from Razer's official product page, read on 19 October 2026, and is written as a buying checklist rather than a review.

## Does the right-handed shape suit how you hold a mouse?

Start here, because no setting can fix a shape that does not fit. Razer's FAQ calls the DeathAdder V3 a right-handed ergonomic shape. The page also describes it as a mid-to-large sized mouse, and says the shape was refined with esports players for palm support and grip.

That points to a clear group of buyers. If you play right-handed and like a mouse that fills the palm, it belongs on your shortlist. If you are left-handed, it is the wrong starting point. A right-handed ergonomic body puts the thumb buttons and the sculpted side on the left, which a left hand cannot use the same way.

If you have small hands or prefer a fingertip grip, treat "mid-to-large" as a warning rather than a deal-breaker. Compare the listed dimensions with the mouse you already own before you order, and keep the retailer's return terms in mind.

## Do you need the 8000 Hz polling rate?

Polling rate is how often the mouse reports its position to the computer. Razer says the DeathAdder V3 can send up to 8000 reports a second, which takes the reporting interval from 1 ms down to 1/8 ms. The default is 1000 Hz, and you raise it in Razer Synapse.

For most players, 1000 Hz is already common and comfortable. The higher setting matters most to competitive players on high refresh-rate monitors who want the newest position data. It also asks more of the computer. Razer's own guidance for testing the polling rate is to plug the mouse straight into a USB port on the PC and close background programs. If your PC is older or busy, plan to test both 1000 Hz and higher settings rather than assuming the maximum is best.

Because this is the wired model, there is no battery or receiver to manage. That is a plus if you want plug-in simplicity, and a minus if you wanted a cable-free desk.

## What can you customise without software running?

The DeathAdder V3 has six configurable buttons, including one that cycles through five DPI stages. Razer says the mouse stores up to five profiles plus the last-used Synapse profile, including DPI, polling rate and lift-off distance.

That matters if you move between computers, such as a home PC and a tournament machine. Your settings travel with the mouse. You still need Razer Synapse 3 to set up button mappings and macros in the first place, according to Razer's FAQ.

The sensor settings go deeper than most players will need. Razer lists 26 levels of asymmetric lift-off and landing distance, a Smart Tracking mode that keeps lift-off consistent across surfaces, and Motion Sync. These are fine-tuning tools, not reasons to buy on their own.

## Which features are missing that you might miss?

Check this list before you buy, because it is where expectations go wrong.

- **No tilt-click scroll wheel.** Razer's FAQ says so directly. If you use sideways scrolling in spreadsheets or bind tilt to game actions, look elsewhere.
- **No wireless.** That is the V3 Pro's job, a separate model.
- **Right hand only.** There is no left-handed version of this shape on the page.

## What do the click and cable specs mean in practice?

Razer rates the optical switches for a 90-million click lifecycle, with no double-clicking issues and 0.2 ms actuation. Optical switches register clicks with light rather than a metal contact, which is why makers claim they avoid the double-click fault that wears out some mechanical switches.

The cable is described as woven for flexibility and low drag. The page also mentions a new smooth-touch coating meant to stay grippy. Neither claim tells you how it will feel in your hand, so if grip texture matters to you, read the retailer's return terms first.

## Before you check out

- Confirm the listing says **DeathAdder V3** and **wired**, not V3 Pro or HyperSpeed.
- Check the colour on the listing matches what you want.
- Make sure your PC has a free USB port you can plug into directly.
- If you plan to use 8000 Hz, confirm you can run Razer Synapse on your computer.
- Read the seller's return window. Razer's page mentions its own return and warranty terms for purchases made directly from RazerStore; other sellers set their own.

## When a different mouse makes more sense

If you are left-handed, want a cable-free desk, or need a tilt wheel, this mouse is not the right buy no matter how good its sensor is. A lighter ambidextrous shape or a wireless model will serve you better.
