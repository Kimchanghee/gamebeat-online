A wireless headset only helps if its receiver fits the device you actually play on. For the black HyperX Cloud III Wireless, the buying question is less about how the headset looks and more about which devices HyperX says its dongle supports. This checklist reads the maker's own pages and the Amazon listing title for the exact variant named above.

We have not used this headset, measured its microphone, or compared its sound with another model. Nothing here is a listening verdict, and the article makes no claim about price, discounts, stock or customer ratings. It covers connection and compatibility only.

## Which devices does HyperX list for the Cloud III Wireless?
HyperX lists PC, PS5, PS4 and Nintendo Switch as the compatible devices. The same list appears in the compatibility line of the technical specs and in HyperX's US announcement, which describes versatile compatibility across those platforms. The Amazon listing title for this black variant names PC, PS5 and PS4 in the product name, so the maker's page is the place to confirm the Switch entry.

Treat that list as the full set of devices you can plan around. A headset that connects through a dongle works only on devices that accept that kind of USB audio device, and the manufacturer's statement is the clearest guide to which ones do.

## Is Xbox covered by the dongle?
Xbox is not on HyperX's compatibility list. The spec sheet names PC, PS5, PS4 and Nintendo Switch, and nothing in the pages we read adds a Microsoft console. We therefore make no claim that this headset works with Xbox Series X|S or Xbox One.

If you play mainly on an Xbox, do not assume that a USB wireless headset will be accepted just because it fits a USB port. Look for a headset that names Xbox in its own compatibility statement. If you are unsure, ask HyperX support before ordering rather than relying on a retailer title.

## What kind of wireless connection does it use?
The listed wireless type is 2.4 GHz, with a stated range of up to 20 meters and a wireless USB audio connection. HyperX describes a USB-C wireless dongle and says a USB-C to USB-A adapter is included, so you can reach either port type. The spec sheet shows USB 2.0 and a stereo USB audio format.

The spec list we read does not show Bluetooth or a 3.5 mm analog connection. That matters if you want one headset for a phone, a laptop and a console without moving a dongle. Plan to carry and plug in the receiver wherever you want to listen. If you need to connect without a receiver, this model's listed connections will not cover that need.

## What do the battery and comfort claims actually promise?
HyperX states up to 120 hours of battery life, and the footnote on its page attaches conditions to that figure. Read the claim as a maker's rating under stated conditions, not as a guarantee for your volume level or use pattern. The page also describes memory foam in the headband and ear cushions, an aluminum frame, and angled 53 mm drivers.

Those are descriptions, not measurements, and none of them tells you how the headset will feel on your head or how loud it sounds. Comfort is personal, so check the seller's return terms for the exact variant. The [safe listening guide](/guides/headphone-safe-listening-volume/) covers volume habits that apply to any headset, wired or wireless.

## What about the microphone and surround sound?
HyperX describes a detachable microphone with an LED mute indicator, and it lists DTS Headphone:X virtual surround. Its page frames the spatial audio as an advantage on PC, so do not assume the same processing runs when the dongle is plugged into a console. Treat the microphone as a hardware feature you can remove, and read the current software requirements separately from the physical connection.

For a broader view of how headset connections work across devices, see the [headset connection guide](/guides/headset-connection-guide/). It explains why the connection type should come before the headset shape or the feature list.

## Who should skip this headset?
Skip it if Xbox is your main console, if you need Bluetooth or a plain 3.5 mm cable, or if you want to share one headset between several devices without moving a receiver. Also skip it if you cannot confirm that your device accepts a USB audio dongle from the HyperX list.

It is a reasonable candidate if you play on one of the listed platforms, want a wireless headset with a long rated battery life, and are comfortable plugging in a dongle each time you switch devices. Write down the device you will use, the port it offers, and whether you need the USB-A adapter before you order. The goal is to match the headset to a known connection, not to buy first and discover an unsupported platform later.
