A capture card sends a copy of your game to a computer, so the card is only half of the purchase. For the Elgato 4K X, the other half is the computer port and software it connects to. A listing headline with 4K144 and HDMI 2.1 does not tell you whether your laptop or desktop can use those numbers.

This checklist uses Elgato's own product page and the exact Amazon product page for the 4K X variant named above. We have not used this capture card, measured latency, or compared recorded footage with another model. It makes no claim about price, discounts, stock or customer ratings, and it is a buying checklist rather than a performance test.

## Which USB port does the Elgato 4K X need?
Elgato lists USB 3.2 Gen 2 as the connection the 4K X requires on both Windows and Mac. The same page says a USB 3.2 Gen 1 port works with reduced capture resolution, so a slower port is not a dead end, but it is not what the listed settings assume. The Amazon title also names USB 3.2 Gen 2.

Port names are confusing on real computers, because the same USB-C connector can sit behind different speeds. Look in your computer's manual or spec sheet for the port speed rather than judging by the shape. If you cannot tell which port is Gen 2, treat the port as unconfirmed and check before ordering.

## What computer and software does Elgato list?
For Windows, Elgato lists Windows 10 (64-bit) or newer, a 6th generation Intel Core i5 or AMD Ryzen 5 or better, and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 10xx or better graphics card. For Mac, it lists macOS 11 or later, an Apple M1 or an Intel i5 4th-generation quad-core or later, a supported AMD, NVIDIA or Apple graphics chip, and 4 GB of RAM.

The software listed on both is Elgato Studio, a free download, and the product page shows the card working as a source in apps such as OBS, Streamlabs Desktop, Twitch Studio and vMix. The page also says you can capture to an iPad with a USB-C port. Compare your own computer line by line with these requirements, and write down any item you cannot confirm.

## Do passthrough and recording limits differ?
Yes, and Elgato's page says so in small print. It lists recording and passthrough resolution up to 4K144, then notes that passthrough is limited to 4K120 on screens that use Display Stream Compression. Capture at lower resolutions and frame rates is always possible, according to the same page.

HDR is handled separately. Elgato says HDR10 capture is currently only available on Windows, and that HDR input can be tone mapped to SDR for capture on the device. So a display that shows HDR and a recording that keeps HDR are two different checks. For the general idea behind that split, see the [capture card HDMI, USB and HDCP guide](/guides/capture-card-hdmi-usb-hdcp/).

## Will your display and console match the card?
The card has an unencrypted HDMI input and an HDMI output for lag-free passthrough, according to Elgato's specifications. The product page names PlayStation 5 and 5 Pro, Xbox Series X, Switch, Steam Deck, ROG Ally, PC, Mac and iPhone among sources it works with, and the Amazon title adds Xbox Series X/S and Switch 2.

Your screen decides what passthrough you can actually see. A 4K144 figure needs a display and cable that can carry it, and our [monitor resolution, refresh and input guide](/guides/monitor-resolution-refresh-input/) explains how to match those three settings. Elgato also publishes a capture quality guide on the product page where you choose a console, resolution and frame rate, which is a better planning tool than the headline alone.

## What is in the box?
Elgato lists the Game Capture 4K X, a USB 3.2 C-to-C cable that is 150 cm long, and an 8K HDMI cable that is 200 cm long. The maker's page gives the unit as 112 x 72 x 18 mm and 91 g. Those are the maker's numbers for the product, not measurements of the exact box from a seller.

If your computer only has USB-A ports, the listed cable is C-to-C, so confirm that you have a USB-C port at the right speed before assuming the box covers it. Do not rely on the listing title for accessories; read the in-the-box line on Elgato's page.

## Who should skip the 4K X?
Skip it if your computer's ports are all slower than USB 3.2 Gen 2 and you want the listed capture settings, or if you want HDR recordings from a Mac. Skip it if you only need to stream from the same PC that runs the game, since a capture card then adds a device without a clear job. It is also not a fit if your display cannot take the signal you want to pass through.

It is a reasonable candidate if you play on a console or handheld, want a copy of the video on a separate computer or iPad, and can confirm the port, computer and software items above. Write down your console, the video settings you plan to use, your computer's port speed and your operating system. If every item matches Elgato's list, the card fits the job; if one does not, fix that first.
