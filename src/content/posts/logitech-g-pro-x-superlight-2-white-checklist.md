The Logitech G PRO X SUPERLIGHT 2 is a wireless mouse built around low weight. Logitech's product page describes it as a 60 g gaming mouse that uses its LIGHTSPEED wireless connection, a HERO 2 sensor and LIGHTFORCE hybrid switches. This checklist is for the White colour.

We have not used this mouse. The facts below come from Logitech G's official product page, read on 19 October 2026. Logitech's page is light on physical dimensions, so we leave out anything it does not state.

## Is a 60 g mouse what you actually want?

Weight is the main reason people choose this model. Logitech calls it a 60 g mouse. Lighter mice are easier to flick and stop quickly, which is why many competitive players in aim-heavy games prefer them.

Light is not automatically better, though. Some players find very light mice harder to hold steady during slow, precise movements. If you currently use a heavy mouse and like its stability, expect an adjustment period. If you already play with a light mouse, this is a smaller step.

## Is wireless the right choice for your desk?

The SUPERLIGHT 2 connects with LIGHTSPEED, Logitech's own wireless technology. That means no cable dragging across your mouse pad. It also means you need to keep it charged and keep the receiver plugged in.

If you play on a laptop with few USB ports, or you often move between machines, check the listing's in-the-box details to see how the receiver and charging cable are supplied. If you want zero battery management, a wired mouse is simpler.

## What do the sensor numbers mean?

Logitech lists the HERO 2 sensor with tracking over 888 IPS and up to 44,000 DPI. IPS is the speed the sensor can follow before it loses tracking. DPI is how far the cursor moves for each inch of hand movement.

Almost nobody plays at 44,000 DPI. Most players use far lower settings and adjust in-game sensitivity. The useful takeaway is that the sensor has plenty of headroom. You will not be limited by it, but a higher number on its own will not improve your aim.

## Will the switches last, and how do they feel?

Logitech says the mouse uses LIGHTFORCE hybrid switches, which combine optical and mechanical parts, rated for 70 million clicks. The aim, according to Logitech, is the speed and reliability of an optical switch with the clicky feel and low power use of a mechanical one.

How a click feels is personal. If you have a strong preference for a soft or crisp click, look for a retailer with an easy return policy.

## Which details should you confirm on the listing?

Because Logitech has sold both the original PRO X SUPERLIGHT and the SUPERLIGHT 2, product titles can be confusing. Before you buy:

- Make sure the title says **PRO X SUPERLIGHT 2**.
- Make sure the colour is **White**, if that is the one you want.
- Check what is in the box on the listing, including the receiver and charging cable.
- Check the seller is reputable and that the return window suits you.

## Who is this mouse for?

It suits right- or left-handed players who want a light, symmetrical-feeling wireless mouse for fast-paced games, and who are happy to charge it. It suits less well if you prefer a heavy, palm-filling ergonomic shape or want lots of side buttons for MMO-style macros.

## How should you set it up the first time?

Before your first match, set a DPI you will stick with and adjust your in-game sensitivity around it. Changing both at once makes it hard to tell what helped. Logitech's page points to its own software and support section for settings and updates, so check there for the current app before you start.

Charge the mouse fully before a long session. With a light wireless mouse, a dead battery mid-game is the most avoidable problem, so build charging into your routine.

## When to pick something else

If you need a right-handed ergonomic body that fills the palm, a model like the wired Razer DeathAdder V3 may fit better. If you want lots of programmable buttons, look at a mouse designed for MMOs.
