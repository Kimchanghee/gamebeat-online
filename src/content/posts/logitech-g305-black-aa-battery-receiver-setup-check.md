A wireless mouse has two parts that decide your day-to-day experience: how it is powered and how it connects. The Logitech G305 answers both in an old-fashioned way, with a replaceable AA battery and a small USB receiver. That is easy to live with if you expect it and annoying if you do not.

This checklist uses Logitech's own specification and product pages and the exact Amazon product page for the Black option named above. We have not used this mouse, timed its battery, or compared its tracking with another model. It makes no claim about price, discounts, stock or customer ratings, and it is a buying checklist rather than a test.

## What does the Logitech G305 need to run?
Logitech lists one AA battery, alkaline or lithium, and says one alkaline battery is included. The battery is replaceable, so there is no charging cable in the listed box contents. Logitech lists battery life as up to 250 hours in performance mode and up to 9 months of standard use in endurance mode, and notes that battery life varies by user, battery and computing conditions.

The mouse connects with a LIGHTSPEED USB receiver rather than a cable. Logitech's system requirements are a USB 2.0 or newer port, plus an internet connection if you want the optional G HUB software. Check that you have a free USB port near where the receiver should sit, since a laptop with only USB-C ports needs its own adapter.

## Which computers and systems are listed?
Logitech lists Windows 7 or later, macOS 10.13 and later, and ChromeOS. The same specification lists only the Logitech G 2.4 GHz wireless protocol with a USB receiver, so we do not describe it as a Bluetooth mouse. Do not assume it pairs directly with a tablet or phone.

If you share a computer between a desk and a travel bag, Logitech says the mouse has built-in storage for the nano receiver, which helps keep it from getting lost. The optional G HUB software is where Amazon's page says endurance mode is switched on, so plan a download if you want that setting.

## How heavy is it, and will it fit your hand?
Logitech lists the mouse at 75 g without the battery and 99 g with one AA battery. The dimensions are 116.6 mm long, 62.15 mm wide and 38.2 mm tall. Those are the maker's numbers for the product, not a verdict on how it feels in your grip.

Weight and shape are personal, and a spec sheet cannot settle them. Our [gaming mouse shape and sensitivity guide](/guides/gaming-mouse-shape-sensitivity/) explains how grip style and hand size interact with a mouse like this. If you currently use a mouse under 70 g, a battery-powered 99 g model will feel different, so measure your hand before you decide.

## What does it offer for buttons and sensor settings?
Logitech lists six programmable buttons, the HERO sensor with 200 to 12,000 DPI, a 1000 Hz wireless report rate and one onboard memory profile. The listed main button durability is 10 million clicks. There is no RGB lighting, and tilt on the scroll wheel is listed as not available.

Advanced button settings need Logitech G HUB, according to the specification. One onboard profile means your settings travel in the mouse, but not several different sets. If you switch between games with different sensitivity needs, plan to adjust with the DPI button or software. Our [monitor resolution and input guide](/guides/monitor-resolution-refresh-input/) helps you think about display settings next to mouse sensitivity.

## Is this the right Black listing?
The Amazon page for this ASIN shows the Black color option and the title Logitech G305 LIGHTSPEED Wireless Gaming Mouse. Logitech's own page lists Black as part number 910-005280, which matches the model number we confirmed. Other colors exist and carry other part numbers, so confirm the color on the page you open.

From our Korea-based browser view, the buy button was not shown, which the page attributes to delivery location. That is a viewing limit, not a claim about US availability, and this checklist makes no stock claim at all.

## Who should skip the G305?
Skip it if you want a rechargeable mouse, a Bluetooth connection to a tablet, several onboard profiles, or a very light mouse under 70 g. Skip it if you have no free USB port and do not want an adapter, or if you dislike changing batteries.

It is a reasonable candidate if you want a simple wireless mouse with a replaceable battery, a receiver you can store inside the mouse, and the listed six buttons. Write down your port type, your operating system, your hand size and the weight you are used to. If every item matches Logitech's list, the mouse fits the job; if one does not, look at another model first.
