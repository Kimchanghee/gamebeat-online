A webcam looks simple until the port behind your monitor decides what the camera can send. The Elgato Facecam MK.2 is sold on 1080p60 video, but the way it connects to your computer changes what you actually get. A listing headline does not tell you whether your desk, port and audio setup match.

This checklist uses Elgato's own product and help pages and the exact Amazon product page for the Facecam style named above. We have not used this webcam, compared its picture with another camera, or measured delay. It makes no claim about price, discounts, stock or customer ratings, and it is a buying checklist rather than a test.

## Which USB port does the Facecam MK.2 need?
Elgato lists USB 3.0 Type-A, or USB 3.0 Type-C with an adapter that is not included, as the connection for the Facecam MK.2. It also lists USB 2.0 ports, with a warning: when connected by USB 2.0, the webcam only supports MJPEG video. The uncompressed resolutions Elgato names, from 1080p60 down to 540p30, are marked as available when connected by USB 3.0.

The Amazon page repeats the same idea in its own wording, noting that USB 2.0 is compressed and USB 3.0 is uncompressed. Look at your computer's manual or spec sheet for port speed instead of judging by the connector shape. If you cannot tell which port is USB 3.0 or better, treat the port as unconfirmed.

## What computer does Elgato list?
For Windows, Elgato lists Windows 10 (64-bit) or newer with an Intel or AMD processor and an Intel, Nvidia or AMD graphics chip. For Mac, it lists macOS 12 or newer with an Intel or Apple processor. The quick start guide tells you to download Elgato's camera app, so plan for a software install.

Elgato adds a separate note for one feature: the built-in background blur and replacement in Camera Hub needs an NVIDIA RTX 20xx or better graphics card on Windows. If that feature matters to you, check your graphics card before ordering. A computer that meets the basic list may still not cover every extra.

## Does the port setup or a hub matter?
Yes. The quick start guide says to connect the Facecam directly to a USB 3.0 or better port on your PC or Mac and not to use a USB hub. That affects desks where every port is already full, or where a dock does the work of several ports.

Write down which port you will use before you buy. If the only free port is on a hub or a dock, you are outside the setup Elgato describes. For help placing a screen and camera at a comfortable height, see our [monitor and desk viewing guide](/guides/monitor-desk-viewing-ergonomics/).

## Will it fit your desk, distance and audio?
Elgato lists a fixed focus range of 30 to 120 cm (11.8 to 47.2 in), a field of view of 84 degrees, and an f/2.4 lens. Fixed focus means there is no autofocus to chase you, so a seat that puts you closer than 30 cm or farther than 120 cm is outside the listed range.

The Facecam has no microphone. Elgato says it leaves audio to your audio devices, and its comparison chart lists the microphone as not included. Budget for a headset or a microphone, and see our [headset connection guide](/guides/headset-connection-guide/) if you are not sure how yours plugs in. The maker's page lists a monitor mount with a 1/4-inch thread, so a tripod or arm that uses that thread can also hold it.

## What about HDR and frame rate?
The listing shows HDR as available up to 1080p30, while 1080p60 is the headline frame rate. Elgato's page describes HDR up to 1080p30, so a setting that gives you HDR is not the same one that gives you 60 frames per second. Decide which you need before buying.

The Amazon page also offers a second style, Facecam 4K, which is a different product. This checklist covers only the style named Facecam, with maker SKU 10WAC9901. If you want to stream a console too, our [capture card HDMI and USB guide](/guides/capture-card-hdmi-usb-hdcp/) covers the other half of that setup.

## Who should skip the Facecam MK.2?
Skip it if your computer only has USB 2.0 ports and you want uncompressed video, or if your only free port is on a hub. Skip it if you sit outside the 30 to 120 cm focus range or want a camera that refocuses on its own. It is also a poor match if you expect a built-in microphone.

It is a reasonable candidate if you stream or make video calls from a desk, have a direct USB 3.0 port and a separate audio device, and sit within the listed distance. Write down your port, your operating system and your seat distance. If every item matches Elgato's list, the webcam fits the job; if one does not, fix that first.
