## Which keys do you actually need within one press?

Make a short list from your real use: game bindings, spreadsheet entry, text navigation, volume adjustment and shortcuts. A keyboard that is pleasant in one game can become frustrating during the rest of the day if an often-used key moves behind a function layer. Start with the keys you need directly, then choose the smallest layout that preserves those priorities.

Corsair's layout guide distinguishes full-size keyboards with a number pad, function row and arrows from tenkeyless models that remove the number pad. That makes TKL a useful starting point when you want more mouse room but still use navigation keys. It is not inherently better for typing or gaming; it is a different allocation of desk space.

If you enter numbers frequently, a number pad may earn its space. If you rarely use it, removing it can bring the mouse closer without moving the letter keys away from a comfortable position. Compare this with the [mouse shape and sensitivity guide](/guides/gaming-mouse-shape-sensitivity/): usable movement space belongs to the whole desk, not to one peripheral in isolation.

## What do 75%, 65% and 60% labels leave out?

Percentage names are shorthand, not complete layout specifications. Corsair notes that exact keys and positions vary, particularly in compact layouts. A 75% model generally retains a function row and arrows while rearranging navigation keys. A 65% model typically keeps arrows but removes the dedicated function row. A conventional 60% layout removes dedicated arrows as well.

Inspect a clear photograph of the exact regional variant. Find Delete, Home, End, the function keys and any shortcut you use without looking. Read the manual's function-layer diagram when a key is absent. A function still existing somewhere in firmware does not mean it will be equally convenient while you are holding another game key.

Try a thought exercise before ordering: take five common tasks and describe the key sequence on the proposed layout. If simple editing becomes a series of unfamiliar chords, decide whether the reclaimed space is worth that trade. Do not buy the smallest case merely because a product photograph makes the desk look clean.

## How do linear, tactile and clicky switches differ?

Corsair defines linear switches as smooth through the press without a tactile feedback bump, tactile switches as having a bump, and clicky switches as combining a bump with an audible click. These categories describe feedback. They do not rank accuracy, comfort or skill, and they do not establish which switch every gamer should buy.

A linear switch is worth considering if you prefer an uninterrupted press. A tactile switch may suit someone who wants a noticeable change during the stroke. A clicky switch makes sound part of that feedback, which deserves discussion if you share a room or keep an open microphone nearby. Listen in the context of the complete keyboard when possible rather than assuming a switch name predicts the whole sound.

Treat a switch sample as a way to compare feedback, not a full keyboard review. Keycaps, enclosure and how you press the keys can affect the experience. Equally, a video recording is not a calibrated comparison of loudness across rooms and microphones. Avoid translating a creator's recording into a guaranteed noise level at your desk.

## Which switch specifications deserve a closer look?

Actuation distance describes the travel to registration, while total travel describes the full stroke. Corsair's switch table lists them separately alongside actuation force. A short registration distance does not mean the key has no further movement, and a lighter force does not automatically mean fewer accidental presses for every person.

Compare the exact switch option offered on the exact keyboard. A family name can cover multiple switches, and color names are not a universal specification across manufacturers. When a listing offers several variants, confirm the selected option again in the cart rather than relying on the title or the first photograph.

For adjustable-actuation products, read what the adjustment actually controls and which software or supported mode it requires. Do not assume that every mechanical keyboard offers this feature. Likewise, hot-swappable does not mean all switch technologies or pin arrangements are interchangeable. Verify the manufacturer's compatible parts before treating future modification as a buying benefit.

## How should layout and workstation comfort work together?

Measure the keyboard's full case width and depth, including any wrist rest you intend to use. A nominally compact model may still have a large bezel, controls or a cable connection that occupies important space. Leave room to move the mouse without repeatedly contacting the keyboard edge.

Place the keyboard where your shoulders can stay relaxed, then check that the chair and desk support that position. The [chair-fit guide](/guides/gaming-chair-fit/) addresses that relationship. A switch purchase cannot fix a workstation that forces you to reach upward or outward all day.

Consider visible legends and lighting controls too. If you regularly use the board in dim conditions, check whether the actual key legends are readable in the configuration you plan to use. Decorative lighting photographs are not proof of legend visibility, and a software-only brightness control may be inconvenient on a machine where that software cannot run.

## What should be on the final buying checklist?

Confirm the regional layout, exact switch variant, connection method, supported operating system and included cable. Save the model identifier rather than only the marketing name. If repairability matters, check replacement keycaps and switch compatibility before purchase rather than assuming parts will be available later.

During the return window, try ordinary writing, your usual game bindings and a short voice-chat recording. Check accidental presses and whether frequently used keys remain easy to reach. These are acceptance steps for the buyer, not a claim of hands-on testing by GameBeat. The best choice is the one whose compromises match your actual tasks and shared environment.
